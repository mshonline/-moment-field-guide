# Part 1. Before you shoot

*Two checklists, one for photo and one for video. Run the one that matches. After a few outings this takes under a minute, and it means nothing has to be decided in the middle of a scene.*

## The photo checklist {updated: 10 Sep}

- [ ] **Lenses are clean** *The edges where glass meets housing collect the most. First thing, before anything on the screen.* {new}
- [ ] **The ND is mounted if you are outdoors** *Dial it until the zebras clear. When the light changes mid-scene, the ND is the first thing to reach for, before EV. No need to go anywhere near its darkest extreme.* {new}
- [ ] **Phone battery checked** *A low battery pauses iCloud sync at the end of the day, and a dead phone ends the shoot.* {new}
- [ ] **Do Not Disturb is on** *Swipe down from the top right corner, tap Focus. A moon appears by the clock. To turn it off, tap the moon itself, not the word Focus.*
- [ ] **The right profile is loaded** *Natural for everyday. ProRAW for contrasty light or anything you might print. The text along the top bar confirms which one you are in.*
- [ ] **Storage says Photo Library, not SD card** *Look at the small icon on the shooting screen. If it shows the card symbol instead of your last image, the location has switched. With Auto Connect now off this should stay put, but it is worth a glance.*
- [ ] **You are in ISO mode, starting around ISO 400** *Pick the ISO, let the app find the shutter. The shutter reading sits just above the shutter button, labelled SS. Bring the ISO down if the light allows.*
- [ ] **EV where you want it** *Zero to start. On Natural, riding at -0.3 to -0.7 protects the highlights, and the raw file holds the shadows.*
- [ ] **White balance is set** *Daylight outside. Auto for genuinely mixed light.*
- [ ] **Autofocus is on, and you can leave it alone** *The lens is wide and the depth of field is deep. Tapping the white box is optional, and mostly worth skipping so you keep both hands on the grip.*
- [ ] **Exposure looks right, no clipping** *The things to watch: a spike against the right edge of the histogram, or a solid white line across the top of the waveform.*
- [ ] **Quick Actions are swept** *Hand icon. Level on, Monitors on, Clipping on at Highlights 99%, Flash off, Timer off, Grid to taste.*
- [ ] **Bluetooth shutter connected, if you are using it**

::: note Why the checklist earns its keep
The app hides a lot behind long presses and nested menus, and mid-scene there is no time to go hunting. Everything on this list can be settled while you are still standing comfortably at the location. Then the shooting is just shooting.
:::

## The video checklist {updated: 10 Sep}

- [ ] **Lenses are clean** {new}
- [ ] **ND is on and dialed in** *At 1/48 in daylight the ND is doing the exposure work. Rotate until the zebras clear, and keep clear of the darkest extreme.* {new}
- [ ] **Do Not Disturb is on**
- [ ] **The 4K profile is loaded** *H.264, 4K, SDR, 24 fps.*
- [ ] **Shutter is 1/48** *Double the frame rate, the 180 degree rule. This is what gives motion its natural look, so video stays in shutter mode even though photo has moved to ISO mode.*
- [ ] **Storage says what you expect** *Same glance as the photo list. Video is usually happier on the SD card given the file sizes, but make it a choice rather than a surprise.*
- [ ] **Stabilization is on**
- [ ] **Audio source is set** *Quick Actions, Monitors on, tap the audio meters. Source: Built-in, then Back microphone, Stereo.*
- [ ] **White balance is set** *Daylight outside, chosen on the shooting screen. Lock Auto White Balance on Record lives in Moment settings (the gear) and is already on, so Auto will not drift mid-clip.* {new}
- [ ] **Exposure checked, no clipping**
- [ ] **Red record border shows when you roll** *Your confirmation that it is really recording. Show Record Border is a Moment setting, already on.* {new}

# Part 2. Your profiles and critical settings

::: note The ten second orientation
Settings live in four places, and knowing which one you are in makes the app much friendlier.

1. The gear icon, lower left. App-wide preferences, set once and rechecked after updates.
2. The profile tab. Saved recipes, switched when you change what you are shooting.
3. The hardware icon, the little hexagon. Storage location and external devices.
4. The shooting screen and Quick Actions. Exposure, white balance, focus, clipping. The scene by scene controls.
:::

## Which profile, and when {updated: 10 Sep}

| Profile | Reach for it when | Good to know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Natural | Your default for everyday shooting. A shareable JPEG plus a raw file, saved together. The raw comes from Keep RAW, which is found by tapping JPEG inside the profile. {new} | The most film-like result the phone gives, but the least highlight headroom, so it clips first. Riding EV a little low covers this. Locked to the physical lens, 12 MP. |
| ProRAW | Contrasty light, indoor or out, and anything that might get printed. | Fusion stacks exposures, so it holds highlights noticeably better. It is HDR, which looks right on your phone but can read differently on other screens, so plan a conversion step when handing files off. |
| 4K Video | Video. | H.264 is SDR with less grading latitude, and in exchange it edits in Premiere without proxies. |

::: warn Low light, for now
A dedicated low light profile is still on the list. Until then: ProRAW on the 1x lens is the stronger choice in dim light, since Fusion stacks exposures in a way Natural cannot.
:::

## Changing a setting and saving it {updated: 10 Sep}

Two different things live under the profile tab, and this is the most useful distinction in the whole app.

### To change a capture setting

*ISO, white balance, clipping, dynamic range, format, resolution.*

1. Load the profile you want to change.
2. Change the setting on the shooting screen, the normal way. Not inside Edit Profile.
3. Tap the profile tab.
4. Tap Save Changes.
5. Open Edit Profile to confirm the new value stuck.

::: note If Save Changes is missing
That just means the profile already matches your current settings. The button only appears when there is something to save.
:::

::: warn Why an unsaved change disappears
This is very likely what happened at Hyannis. You raised the ISO to 400 mid-shoot because the shutter was too slow, but did not save it, and some files came back at ISO 80. Auto-save is deliberately off, so any change you make in the field lives only until the profile is loaded again. Then the saved value quietly takes over. Both profiles are now saved at 400, which removes the problem: your starting point is high enough that any adjustment you make will be downward, and a lost adjustment leaves you no worse off than where you began.
:::

::: warn If a value on the shooting screen looks wrong
Marnie's Natural profile showed ISO 125 after tapping around the ISO control, with 400 saved. Switching to another profile and back reloaded the saved 400. Any time a number looks off, that switch is the quick check: the profile always comes back with what was saved.
:::

### Edit Profile is for the rest

Auto-save, storage location, and viewfinder items live inside Edit Profile, which is also where you look to confirm what a profile currently holds. Close it with the X in the black panel at the top.

- Auto-save stays off, per profile, so field tweaks never quietly rewrite your saved starting points.
- Storage location is saved per profile and set under the hardware icon, the hexagon opposite the gear. File Name Format is not per profile: it lives in the gear settings under Photo and applies everywhere. {new}
- Photo and video profiles are separate by design. Settings do not carry across.
- Profiles are not available while the phone is locked. Unlock first, then load.

## Where your shots are being saved

This one caused real trouble on the Hyannis shoot, and it turned out not to be operator error at all.

### What was happening

Your profile was set to the photo library. Partway into shooting, the little icon changed to the card symbol and images were going to the SD card instead. You had not touched anything.

The cause: a setting called Auto Connect, under the hardware icon. It tells the app to switch to external storage automatically whenever an external device appears. So the moment the phone slid into the grip with a card in it, Moment quietly redirected everything to the card.

### The fix, done in the session

1. Tap the hardware icon, the small hexagon.
2. Tap Location.
3. Under External Storage, turn Auto Connect off.
4. Set the location back to Photo Library.
5. Tested by removing the phone from the grip and reinserting it. The location stayed on Photo Library.

::: note Choosing the card on purpose
With Auto Connect off, nothing stops you using the card, it just becomes a deliberate choice. Go to the hardware icon, then Location, and select the card. Video especially is often better off there given the file sizes. The card symbol on the shooting screen is your confirmation. It is a small, easy thing to miss, which is exactly why it went unnoticed for most of a shoot. A glance at it belongs on the checklist now.
:::

# Part 3. In the field

## Exposure: ISO priority for photo

The reason for this mode. With an aperture this wide, outdoor shutter speeds land in the thousands, where choosing between 1/4000 and 1/8000 means nothing. So set the ISO and let the app calculate the shutter.

| | The setting |
| --- | --- |
| Mode | ISO, in both Natural and ProRAW. |
| Starting ISO | 400, saved into both profiles. |
| Why 400 rather than lower | Your own reasoning from the Hyannis shoot, and it is sound: coming down from a higher ISO is easy, while discovering mid-shoot that the shutter is too slow to hand hold costs you frames. With the ND on, a low ISO drops the shutter further than you want. |
| Where the shutter reads | Just above the shutter button, labelled SS. In ISO mode that number is feedback rather than a control. |
| Your exposure lever | Outdoors, the ND first. When the sun comes out and the zebras appear, a twist of the ring is quicker than tapping the screen, the same reflex as on a Sony. EV is the fine control after that. Let the shutter fall where it falls. |

::: note The one time shutter mode comes back for photo
Deliberate motion blur. To let a crowd smear, switch to shutter mode, drag down to something like 1/15, and use the ND to hold the exposure. That is the one case where the shutter number is a creative choice, and it is a lovely effect worth playing with.
:::

## Focus

Worth trusting the camera here. On the Hyannis shoot you stopped tapping the white box and let the camera choose, and everything came back sharp.

- The lens is wide and the aperture is fixed, which gives deep depth of field. Most of the frame is in focus whether you intervene or not.
- Tapping means taking a hand off the grip, and by the time you have tapped, the subject has usually moved.
- The exception is close work. If you are near your subject, depth of field shrinks and it is worth tapping to place focus deliberately.

## Reading the histogram, waveform, and zebras

Tap the histogram to switch to the waveform. They answer different questions.

| | Histogram | Waveform |
| --- | --- | --- |
| What it tells you | How much of the image is blown out. | Which part of the frame is blown out. Left of the display is left of your frame. |
| The warning sign | A spike against the right edge. | A solid white line along the top. |
| Looking good | Distribution inside the frame, not pinned to either edge. | Mostly in the middle band. |

- Which to use: the histogram for the quick in-the-moment check. If it is not piled against either edge, you are safe enough to shoot. The waveform earns its place when you can do something about one part of the frame, such as a window that is too bright.
- Zebras live in Quick Actions under Clipping, in both photo and video. Press and hold anywhere on the Clipping square to change the threshold: Highlights at 99%, shadows off.
- At 99% the zebras only fire at the true limit. A few sparkles on water or a specular highlight are fine to leave. Zebras spreading across a face or a sky you want texture in are the cue to pull back.
- Both displays are small and unlabelled. That is the app, not you.

## The variable ND filter

The aperture is fixed, so the ND is your only way to cut light without touching shutter or ISO. Outdoors it goes on for stills as well as video.

- For video at 1/48, the ND does the exposure work. Dial it until the zebras clear.
- For photo in ISO mode, it keeps the whole system in a comfortable range on bright days, and it is what makes slow-shutter motion blur possible in daylight.
- It is also the first move when the light changes. Zebras appear, twist the ring. EV is the finer adjustment once the ND has done the heavy lifting.

::: warn Keep away from the darkest extreme
Your test confirmed it: cranked to maximum and panned across the horizon, artifacts appear. It did not take much backing off for them to clear, so the working range is everything short of the last stretch of the ring. At moderate density the artifact is subtler on the monitor, so when you have been near the extreme, ease off a touch more than looks necessary.
:::

## Contrasty light: ProRAW first

Confirmed side by side: pointed at a bright sky, ProRAW holds the highlights noticeably better than Natural, because Fusion is stacking exposures while Natural works from a single frame.

| Situation | The move |
| --- | --- |
| Contrasty light, indoor or outdoor | ProRAW with HDR. The default for high contrast, not just for shots headed to print. |
| Natural, when you want that look | Ride EV at -0.3 to -0.7. The sensor holds shadows far better than highlights, and the raw file is there for lifting them later. So far every shoot has been ProRAW, so Natural's look is still an unknown; worth a side by side of the same scene to settle it. |
| Video | 4K profile, ND dialed in, watch the zebras. |

::: warn Still unsettled: Light versus Strong
Natural's dynamic range control behaved differently on the two phones: Strong reduced clipping on one and changed nothing visible on the other. Light remains the setting for now, and this is worth a more controlled look sometime.
:::

## Blocking interruptions

### Messages

Swipe down from the top right corner, tap the moon labelled Focus. The moon by the clock confirms it, and reminds you to turn it off after. Messages still arrive, they just stop dropping banners over your viewfinder.

To turn it off, tap the moon itself. Tapping the word Focus next to it opens the list of Focus modes instead, which is where the confusion came from.

### The camera roll stuck in vertical

If the camera roll views only in portrait while the viewfinder rotates fine, that is the iPhone rotation lock. Moment's viewfinder overrides it, the roll does not. Swipe down from the top right and tap the padlock, which sits right above the moon. It should normally be off.

### Sharing several images at once

Moment's camera roll is a window onto the same photo library, and it does not allow selecting several images to AirDrop or send. For that, switch to the Photos app, select, and share from there.

## When something goes wrong

| | How | Use when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Force quit the app | Swipe up from the bottom a little way and pause before you let go, then swipe the app card up and away. It is a small nudge with a pause at the end, then a big swipe. | First thing to try. Fast and harmless. |
| Restart the phone | Swipe down from the top right, press and hold the power symbol, slide to power off. Or hold the side button and either volume button until the slider appears. | An app is stuck and force quitting did not clear it. This is what resolved the stuck-recording episode. |
| Force restart | Press and release volume up, press and release volume down, then hold the side button until the Apple logo appears. | The phone itself is frozen. |

# Part 4. After the shoot: the media workflow

## The shape of it {updated: 10 Sep}

::: note One session folder holds the whole shoot
Everything from a shoot day, photo and video, from the camera roll and from the SD card, lands inside that day's Capture One session folder on the primary drive. The session is where all the work happens, and it is what gets backed up. The archive drive comes later, and separately: a plain copy of the trimmed raw captures, the finished copyrighted files, the final TIFF, and the original DNGs of the finals, in folders anyone could navigate without Capture One. {new}
:::

The work after a shoot falls into three chunks, and only the first one belongs to the end of the day. {new}

1. End of day. Get the originals into two places, check they are all there, then clear the phone and the card. Nothing else. {new}
2. Editing. Rate, cull, export, copyright, and keep the backup drive current with ChronoSync. Days or weeks later, in as many sittings as it takes. {new}
3. Archive. A finishing step, when the session is done: drag the trimmed captures and the Output folder to the archive drive. {new}

## Before the shoot: set up the session

1. In Capture One, create a new session on the iPhone Capture One Sessions Primary drive, named with the date and event: 20260813_EVENT_SESSION.
2. Rename the session subfolders with the same full prefix: the capture folder, Selects, Output, and Trash.
3. In Finder, inside the capture folder, make two subfolders, also carrying the full name: one for the camera roll, one for the SD card.
4. The full date and event prefix on every folder means any folder that gets moved or copied still says exactly what it is.
5. Doing this before the shoot means the folders are waiting for you at the end of a long day.

## End of day, step by step {updated: 10 Sep}

*Chunk one. The only goal is two copies of everything, then a clear phone and a clear card for tomorrow.*

1. On the laptop, open Photos and let the day finish syncing from iCloud. The laptop needs to be plugged in, and check the phone's battery too, since a low battery pauses sync. {new}
2. Select the day's shots and use File, Export, Export Unmodified Originals, into the camera roll capture subfolder. Unmodified is the word to look for; the plain Export makes converted copies. {new}
3. Copy the SD card's contents into the SD card subfolder. Everything together, photos and video, then compare sizes with the card to confirm the copy is complete.
4. In Capture One, batch rename everything, video included: control click, Batch Rename, find and replace the prefix with the date and event, 20260813_EVENT_. That is copy number one, named. {new}
5. In Finder, drag the whole session folder, the parent with everything in it, onto the iPhone Capture One Sessions Backup drive. A plain drag, not ChronoSync. That is copy number two. {new}
6. Now it is safe to clear the phone: delete the day's images in Photos on the laptop, which also removes them from iCloud and the phone in one move. Select, delete, confirm. There is no trash to empty.
7. Clear the card: with the card in the laptop, select everything on it, Command Delete, then empty the Trash while the card is still in the slot. For this card that is all formatting means; there is no camera folder structure to rebuild. {new}

::: warn One small check during the rename
The prefix to replace can vary. Photos come in as IMG. Video names use the long timecode format regardless of your settings, so glance at what the clips are actually called and set the find-and-replace to match.
:::

::: warn The order matters in one place
Two copies before anything gets deleted. Everything else in the sequence has some give, but step 5 always comes before steps 6 and 7. {new}
:::

## Editing the session {updated: 10 Sep}

*Chunk two. Whenever you sit down to it, in as many sittings as it takes.* {new}

1. Rate as usual. The 4 and 5 stars are what gets exported. {new}
2. Cull the no-go images. Delete the ones you will never use, so the capture folder becomes the trimmed set that goes to the archive later. {new}
3. Tone in Capture One as usual. {new}
4. Export with both recipes ticked: Edit for Archive TIFF C1 and Edit for Archive Original C1. One export, two subfolders in Output, a TIFF and its DNG side by side. The next section has the recipe setup. {new}
5. Run the copyright pass in Photo Mechanic on the Output folders, originals included, then the Finder rename. The copyright section covers it. {new}
6. Run the ChronoSync task, iPhone Capture One Sessions Primary to Backup, so the edits and exports land on the backup drive too. Run it whenever you have done substantial work, and always at the end. {new}

### The two export recipes {new}

Bob Korn does not work from edits; he wants the DNG. So every finished image should travel with its original, and Capture One can do that in the same export. {new}

- The TIFF recipe (David's, renamed): output subfolder Edit for Archive TIFF C1. {new}
- The originals recipe (new, made with the plus sign under the recipes list): type Copy original files, output subfolder Edit for Archive Original C1, and Include adjustments unchecked. Pure original, nothing baked in. {new}
- Tick both recipes, select the images, export once. The Output folder gets two subfolders with matching names: 1754.tif in one, 1754.dng in the other. {new}
- Without this, finding the DNG for a finished image means digging through the captures folder. With it, the pair is always together. {new}

::: warn Include adjustments, unchecked
The originals recipe only does its job with Include adjustments off. If it is on, the "original" is a processed copy and Bob will not want it. Worth a glance each time until it is habit.
:::

## Copyright, before anything goes out {updated: 10 Sep}

Capture One does not reliably embed your copyright on export. It can look as though it is there when it is not. Photo Mechanic does it properly, so it becomes the last step before any file leaves your hands.

### Part one: embed the copyright

1. In Capture One, tick both recipes, TIFF and Original. Check that the four boxes at the top of the Capture One window are orange, if your version shows them. {new}
2. Export the images into the session Output folder. You get a TIFF subfolder and an Original subfolder. {new}
3. Open Photo Mechanic.
4. In Finder, go to the session Output folder and find the subfolders, TIFF and Original. {new}
5. Drag one folder at a time onto the Photo Mechanic icon in the dock. The originals get the same pass as the TIFFs. {new}
6. Command A to select all.
7. Command I to open the IPTC window. It is also under Image in the top menu.
8. Click Load to find your copyright template. In Desktop Working Files, open PHOTO MECHANIC TEMPLATE from DEK, then in the next column choose MCS2026_IPTC.XMP and click Open.
9. Scroll down to Image Rights and confirm it looks right.
10. Click Apply Template to Selected.

### Part two: verify it took

Bring one image into Photoshop, then File, File Info. The metadata appears there. If a field looks cut off in the display, select all and copy it into a note to see the full text. That truncation is a Photoshop display quirk, not missing data.

### Part three: tag the filenames

So that a copyrighted file announces itself even after it has been moved or pulled into a group of selects.

1. Still in the session Output folder, in Finder, select all the files you just processed.
2. Right click any one of them and choose Rename.
3. In the format dropdown at the top left, change it from Format to **Add Text**.
4. Type your tag, with a leading underscore: _C2026. Leave the position set to **after the name**, which is the default. Finder shows a preview of the new name at the bottom of the panel before you commit.
5. Click **Rename**. Command Z undoes it if something looks wrong.
6. Rename the enclosing folder by hand to note the copyright as well.

::: note Why the rename happens here and not earlier
The tag should only ever appear on files that genuinely carry the copyright, so it goes on after Photo Mechanic has written the metadata, never before. Doing it in Capture One at rename time would put the tag on files that had not been through the copyright step yet. And doing it while the files are still in the session means they arrive on the archive drive already named and already carrying their metadata. Nothing to redo on the other side.
:::

*The plain-text symbol C works better than the © character, which can occasionally cause trouble in filenames.*

## Your drives {updated: 10 Sep}

| Drive | Role |
| --- | --- |
| iPhone Capture One Sessions Primary | The working drive, and where all the real work lives. One session per shoot, holding originals, edits, and outputs (including final TIFF). |
| iPhone Capture One Sessions Backup from Primary | The mirror. The first copy of a fresh session is the end-of-day drag; after that, ChronoSync keeps it current. Because the sessions hold the edits, this is the backup that matters most. {new} |
| iPhone Raw Captures Archive Primary | The long view. Trimmed raw captures, the copyrighted JPEGs and TIFFs, the original DNGs, and the final TIFF, in plain folders. Filled by hand, never by ChronoSync. {new} |

### The ChronoSync task

- Named iPhone Capture One Sessions Primary to Backup. Left side: the primary drive with Admin Access. Right side: the backup drive with Admin Access. Backup left to right. Synchronize deletions and archive deletions, both ticked. {new}
- Sync the whole drive, not one session at a time. ChronoSync only moves what is new or different, so untouched sessions cost nothing, and you never have to pick the right folder. {new}
- Run it after an edit session so ratings, toning, and exports flow across, and whenever you have done substantial work you would not want to lose. It only ever runs between the two sessions drives. The archive drive is a manual drag. {new}
- If you have culled originals in the session, the sync removes them from the backup too, into ChronoSync's own archive of deletions. That is what "archive deletions" means. {new}
- If it ever throws errors on individual files, check whether the target drive is full before suspecting anything worse. ChronoSync's own archive of old versions is usually what fills it. From the top menu choose Actions, Archive, Clean Up, and choose how much of the archive to delete.

## The archive drive, separately {updated: 10 Sep}

*Chunk three. A finishing step, when the session is done. It can happen at the end of a project, or whenever it suits.*

### What goes over, and how

1. In Finder, make a parent folder on the archive drive named for the date and event. {new}
2. Drag the capture folder from the session into it. By now it is the trimmed set, with the no-go images already culled, camera roll and SD card subfolders as they were, video included. {new}
3. Drag the Output folder into it as well: the copyrighted JPEGs, the copyrighted TIFFs, and the Original DNGs, which were copyrighted at the same time. {new}
4. Include the final TIFF, if there is one. {new}
5. What stays behind in the session: the Trash folder, the Capture One settings, and anything else that only means something inside Capture One. {new}

::: note What the archive is for
The sessions drive holds the work. The archive holds something different: files a person could open in twenty years without owning Capture One. That is also the argument for the DNGs and a large TIFF of your finished images, since both will still open long after any particular application has come and gone. It also means your original captures exist in three places once a shoot is archived: the session, the session backup, and here.
:::

- Exhibition prints and finals, the ones Bob Korn makes, belong on the archive drive in their own area, apart from working sessions. Right now the final exhibit files are stored separately, one place per project (Outer Cape, for example), and whether that stays the arrangement is still open. The argument for it: all the finals of a project in one place, rather than hunting image by image through sessions.
- Copyright registration for finals, and how the Copyright Office's naming rules link back to your own names, is still on the list to work out.

## Cleaning up an older session {updated: 10 Sep}

Older sessions have the Moment naming, no culling, and sometimes no copyright. A recipe, so it is not a fresh decision every time. {new}

::: note First decide: fix it, or start over
If little or no editing was done in the old session, it is cleaner to make a new session and bring the captures in fresh. If it holds real work, ratings and toning and exports, keep it; the exports are already there and only need the copyright pass. {new}
:::

1. Rename the session and its folders with the date and event prefix, the way a new session is set up. {new}
2. Select all the captures and Batch Rename: replace the Moment prefix with the date and event, and keep the long string at the end as the image's ID. {new}
3. Look at the ratings. Cull the no-go images. {new}
4. Check the exports. If the good ones are already exported, they only need step 5. If not, export with both recipes. {new}
5. Copyright pass and Finder rename on the 4 and 5 stars, originals included. {new}
6. Archive as usual. {new}

::: warn Select All did not work in one converted session
In one old session, converted from a previous Capture One version, Command A and Edit, Select All did nothing, on both the hand tool and the arrow. A newer session worked fine. Unexplained for now. If it happens, that session is a candidate for starting over. {new}
:::

## Why these formats, in plain terms

*Background only. Here for the moment you wonder why a choice was made.*

| Format | What it actually is | What that means for you |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Natural | Moment's own tone mapping on the raw sensor data, without Apple's sharpening or noise reduction. | The most film-like, closest to a straight capture. A single exposure, so it clips first, which is why EV rides a little low. |
| ProRAW | A 16-bit DNG blending raw sensor data with Apple's computational stacking. | The best editing latitude and highlight handling on the phone, and the only route to 48 MP. Big files. |
| H.264 video | The older codec, 4:2:0, SDR only. | Less grading room than HEVC, and it edits in Premiere without proxies, which is the trade you chose. The Sony a7 III shipped with it for years. |

### HDR and SDR, stated plainly

HDR looks right on your iPhone and can read differently on someone else's screen. SDR is safer for handoff. But 48 MP requires HDR, and resolution is the reason to reach for ProRAW, so ProRAW stays HDR and Natural, where most shooting happens, stays SDR.

## Phone and Mac housekeeping {updated: 10 Sep}

*Not camera work, but it came up and you wanted it written down.* {new}

### Messages that say Not Delivered {new}

Green messages are SMS: the Mac sends them through your iPhone, so the phone has to be online and nearby (in the house is fine, in the car with you is fine, in Boston while you are on the Cape is not). Blue messages are iMessage, sent over the internet from either device on its own. A green failure usually means the phone was not reachable; a blue failure to someone with an iPhone is more likely a stale session between the Mac and your Apple ID. {new}

The recipe, done on 10 September: {new}

1. On the Mac, open Messages, then Messages, Settings in the top menu. {new}
2. Click the iMessage tab, the one with the @ symbol, in the middle of the top row. {new}
3. Click Sign Out. {new}
4. Sign back in with your Apple ID and password. Both are in 1Password. {new}
5. Send yourself a test message and confirm it arrives on both the Mac and the phone. {new}

Two things to check on the phone if it recurs: Settings, Apps, Messages, Text Message Forwarding, where the MacBook Pro should be on (the second phone listed there can stay off). And in that same iMessage tab on the Mac, "You can be reached at" still lists a long-gone iPad; harmless, but it can be removed. {new}

::: warn Give it a few weeks
Signing out and in refreshes the connection and often clears the Not Delivered problem, but it is a wait-and-see fix. If it keeps happening, especially blue to blue, that is worth a session of its own. {new}
:::

### The photo library and space {new}

- Space is not the problem. The phone is a half terabyte and the library is a small fraction of that. Photos are close to a rounding error; only video adds up. {new}
- Personal photos, family, documents, the license plate shot: those stay in the iPhone library. That is the trade-off of shooting on the phone you also live on, and it is fine. {new}
- Thin the throwaways on the laptop: select in Photos, delete, confirm. That removes them from the phone too. {new}
- Tennis lesson videos go to the tennis drive. They are the one thing that takes real room. {new}
- Family photos do not need Capture One sessions. If you want a copy on the family drive, select them in Photos, then File, Export, Export Unmodified Originals, into a folder on that drive. Unmodified is the word to look for. {new}
- Dragging photos into a shared album (Crawford Shenanigans) does not move them; they stay in your library and are also shared. {new}

### The SD card, the habit {new}

Empty it every time, once the shoot is safely in two places, so the next outing never starts with "is there something on this card I need?" The moves are in step 7 of the end of day sequence: select all, Command Delete, empty the Trash with the card still in the slot. {new}
